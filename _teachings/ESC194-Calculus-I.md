## Course Overview

This course covers an introduction to calculus and some real analysis. It builds calculus from the ground up, starting with the rigorous definition of the limit. Topics covered include:

- Functions
- Limits
- ε-δ definition of the limit
- Continuity
- Derivatives
- Applications of Differentiation
- Integrals
- Applications of Integration
- Inverse Functions
- Intro to Differential Equations

## Textbooks

- Primary: "Calculus" by James Stewart
- Reference: "Calculus" by Tom Apostol
- Reference: "Some Foundations of Analysis for Engineering Science" by E.J. Barbeau, P.C. Stangeby, and J.W. Davis

## Personal Comments

If you want to learn this material properly, this will be your most time consuming class. I recommend getting a physical copy of Stewart Calculus and working from there -- Treat it as your holy book! Personally, I skipped most of my lectures in favour of studying from the textbook. I recommend doing as many problems as possible. The first few weeks of lecture are fairly slow paced, but I would recommend against skipping lectures on epsilon-delta, as that is a particularly tricky topic. Once Prof. Davis takes over, the pace of the course picks up tremendously! Be prepared to cover multiple textbook sections in a lecture! Make sure you review tomorrow's content before tomorrow's lecture. Use your first few weeks of the semester to work as far ahead as you can! There is no such thing as being too far ahead in this course! When midterms come up, grind out as many of the previous years' midterms as possible, and review *all* the proofs.
